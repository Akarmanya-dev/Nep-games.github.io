तुइन

Tuin · a river crossing
  1. Going down: the cable sags, so you slide toward the middle. Hold to brake.
  2. Going up: tap to pull yourself along. Hold to grip and rest your arms.
  3. The seat swings. Pull while it swings forward to calm it.
  4. Swing too much and passengers get scared, and goods drop in the river. 3 mistakes end the day.

Deliver as much as you can before sunset.

कसरी खेल्ने?

How to play

Going down: hold to brake

The cable sags in the middle, so the first half is downhill. Hold to grip the cable and slow down. Arrive at the bottom too fast and the seat swings hard.

Going up: tap to pull

Past the middle, tap to pull yourself along the cable. Let go completely and you roll back toward the middle. Hold to grip and rest your tired arms (the bar next to you).

Watch the swing

The seat swings under the pulley, and every tug kicks it backward. Pull while the seat swings forward and the swing calms down. Pull at the wrong moment and it gets wilder. Wind and a wriggling goat don't help.

Passengers and goods

Passengers (a student, a grandmother, a goat) are heavy and get scared by big swings or high speed: you must stop and calm them. Goods (rice, gas, medicine, mail) can drop into the river. Each counts as a mistake. 3 mistakes end the day.

Before sunset

Deliver as many loads as you can before the sun goes down (the bar at the top). Each crossing brings a new load back the other way.

About tuins: in remote parts of Nepal, a tuin is often the only way across a river, for schoolchildren, patients and goods alike. Since 2015 Nepal has replaced most of the tuins it counted with suspension bridges, but about 60 places in Karnali and Sudurpashchim still depend on them (Kathmandu Post, 2026).
